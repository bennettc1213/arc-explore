# Build Roadmap — Scholarship & Internship Platform

Seven phases, ordered so the core matching loop (Phase 02) is live before anything else gets built on top of it. Work through phases in order — each one should be functional before starting the next.

**Status legend:** `[x]` done · `[~]` partly done, see note · `[ ]` not started.
Notes in _italics_ record what was actually built and where it differs from the original line, so the difference is deliberate rather than forgotten.

  **Row count is the wrong metric, and measuring it changed what to build next.** Data density by source, open rows only: Scholarships.com carries an amount on **0%**, a deadline on **0%** and eligibility on **0%** of its 1,559 rows — its listing endpoint returns a name and a URL and nothing else — while the direct-scraped UNL carries amount and deadline on **100%** of 259. So 86% of the corpus was a title and a link: unscoreable on the Fit Score's award dimension, invisible to the deadline filter, unusable for Phase 05 reminders. Enriching it is not an option — Parse detail endpoints cost a credit each, so 1,559 rows against a 200/month budget is arithmetically impossible. **Depth comes from direct scrapes, not from more aggregator rows.** Communities Foundation of Texas (48 endowed institutional funds, all past-deadline right now), University of Nebraska–Lincoln's external list (264, ~259 genuinely open), Scholarships.com (1559 US listings across 8 curated directory sections, live-verified and persisted 2026-08-14 00:21), and University of Nevada, Reno's external list (59 entries, added 2026-08-14 on the strength of the density finding above — `robots.txt` is `Disallow:` with an empty value, permitting everything). **UNR was picked for what it states, not how many rows it has: eligibility prose on 100% of entries**, which no other source gives us at all, and which is exactly the text `fieldsFromDegreeLanguage` reads. Its rows are field-classifiable at 32% against Scholarships.com's 4%. Amounts land on 39% and deadlines on 10%, both parsed out of prose and left null wherever `parseAmount` is not confident; a deadline stated without a year ("deadline of March 22") rolls forward to the next occurrence rather than resolving into the past. **ScholarshipPortal (3666 rows) has never persisted** — three consecutive attempts died: a pooler `CONNECTION_CLOSED` after a 17-minute crawl, an insert failure whose real cause was unreadable, then a 429. Two fixes landed since (see below); the third attempt is gated on Parse credits, not on code. Verified before writing any code: the direct-scrape candidates (scholarships.com, niche.com, bold.org, CareerOneStop) are contractually banned, WAF-blocked, or structurally empty behind a gated portal — so Scholarships.com and ScholarshipPortal were brought in through the **Parse scraping API**, a licensed wrapper, instead of being scraped directly (`lib/scholarships/parse.ts`; metered at **200 credits/month** on the free tier — not the 100/day this line used to say, and the distinction matters: one full ScholarshipPortal crawl is ~19 pages and therefore ~19 credits, so a failed attempt costs ~10% of the month's budget and "wait for tomorrow" is the wrong advice. The 429 message no longer names a reset window it cannot know). University/foundation financial-aid pages are what actually worked direct. See README for the bugs only a live run surfaced — unstable pagination faking closures, a source typo parsing as a confident $0, re-closing already-closed rows, Supabase pooler dropping connections mid-persist (`idle_timeout: 55` plus batched upserts), and an uncapped `Retry-After` sleep that made a 429 hang for hours (now capped at 60s). **Two more found 2026-08-14 while diagnosing why ScholarshipPortal never landed:** the `posting_sources` insert was never batched — the `postings` upsert beside it was, with a comment explaining exactly why — so every row went in one statement, the shape that drops a pooler connection; and every ingest failure was recorded as `err.message`, which for a Drizzle error is the whole statement plus every bound parameter, burying the actual Postgres cause on `err.cause`. That is why the 00:52 failure logged ~300KB and still could not be diagnosed. `lib/ingest/errors.ts` now walks the cause chain, pulls the SQLSTATE and constraint off it, drops the params (they can hold user data) and caps the result at 400 chars. **Open issue:** the UNL crawl still intermittently drops a row (263/263/264 across three consecutive scrapes), which closes a live scholarship until the next run picks it up again; the real fix is requiring absence from two consecutive scrapes, not another sort key. Rows now also carry `amount_needs_review` (source stated a figure we could not parse, as distinct from stating none) and `is_content_marketing` (small-award law-firm link-building scholarships — 126 of 314, tagged not filtered, for the scholarship Fit Score to weigh later)._

## Phase 02 — Core Matching MVP
*One profile, one feed, one honest score. This is the actual product — everything else layers on top of it.*

- [x] Build the student profile intake form (major, GPA, grad year, location, interests, eligibility flags)
- [x] Build the combined browse/search feed — scholarships and internships together, one list
  _One shared `postings` table, one feed, one rank. `getFeed` returns both kinds and the in-memory ranking runs before the limit so one kind cannot crowd out the other. Text search added: AND-of-substrings over title, org/sponsor and eligibility, pushed into SQL because it is the one filter that can cut the row count by orders of magnitude and everything downstream costs per row. `descriptionText` is deliberately not searched — "python" sits in the boilerplate of half our internship descriptions, so including it makes search look broken by matching nearly everything. `ilike`, not `tsvector`: at ~4k rows the scan is free and "eng" should find "Engineering", which stemming would break. Wildcards in the query are escaped (`feed-search.ts`) — unescaped, a search for "100%" matched every row in the table._
- [x] Add filters: deadline, amount/pay, category, location, remote
  _`kind`, `deadline` (days out), `minAmount`, and `location` added; term, remote and show-closed existed. Category now added — and deliberately **not** backed by `postings.category`, which is null on all 3,765 rows and would have filtered to zero forever (`organizations.vertical` is equally empty). It reuses the six-key field taxonomy the Fit Score already matches on, via the same exported functions the scorer calls, so a row can never show a "matches software" chip and be missing from the software category. Applied in memory for that reason: restating the regexes in SQL would create a second copy to drift. **The honest cost:** the taxonomy can only read what a source wrote, and 90% of open scholarships (1,632 of 1,820) name no field at all, so the feed reports that count beside the results rather than letting a category look like an empty corpus._
- [x] Design the scholarship Fit Score formula (eligibility match, competition-level heuristic, essay/effort required)
- [x] Implement the scholarship Fit Score and show it on every listing
  _Three dimensions: field (35, degree-language match over title/sponsor/eligibility), award (35, tiered by stated amount), competition (30, the `is_content_marketing` tag). Unknown is dropped, never a miss, same contract as the internship score._
- [x] Design the internship Fit Score approach (skills/keyword match against the listing description)
- [x] Implement the internship Fit Score and show it on every listing
  _Five weighted dimensions: work authorization, term, field, location, skills. Unknown dimensions are dropped from the average, never scored as a miss._
- [x] Add a "why this score" breakdown so a student can see what drove the number
  _Plus an `N/5` marker, because a posting known on one dimension can otherwise hit 100 and look as confident as one known on all five. Ranking shrinks toward neutral by confidence for the same reason._
- [x] Add a "posted X hours/days ago" freshness badge to every listing
  _Reads "confirmed live 5h ago" — what we last verified on the employer's own board, not a posting date we were told._

  _Automated browser walkthroughs pass. No real students yet._

## Phase 03 — Resume & Cover Letters
*Every material a student submits with an application, grounded in the same match data as the Fit Score, not generated in a vacuum.*

- [x] Build the resume editor (education, experience, skills, projects)
  _`/resume` — edits the structure the parser produced, which is what every score and both generators actually read. Posted as one JSON payload rather than index-encoded form names (`experiences.0.role`), because deleting the second of three rows leaves a hole that server-side reindexing has to paper over; the server still validates against the same `parsedResumeSchema` the parser's output goes through, so an edit cannot put anything in the column the parser could not have — including its 60-skill and 20-experience caps. Cleared fields become **null, never `""` or `0`**: `Number("")` is 0, and a GPA stored as 0.0 reads as a real and very bad GPA rather than an absent one. Skills and bullets stay raw text while typing and split only on submit, or a student cannot type a comma. **The provenance note in `resume/types.ts` was rewritten rather than left to rot:** it said a parsed resume records only what the document says, which an editor breaks. The rule was always about *us* not inventing facts, not about a person correcting our reading of their own life — the generators still assert only what is present in the structure, with the student vouching for it instead of an extractor. **Not verified end-to-end:** auth is magic-link only, so the signed-out redirect and the decode logic are tested but the rendered editor has not been driven with a real session._
- [~] Add resume import — upload an existing PDF/DOCX, or paste from LinkedIn
  _PDF and plain text. DOCX is a zip of XML we cannot read without another dependency — the UI says so rather than failing oddly. No LinkedIn paste._
- [x] Parse resume content into structured fields the matching engine can read
  _PDFs go to Claude as a document block, not through a text extractor: two-column resumes interleave into nonsense otherwise. Absent means null, always._
- [x] Connect resume content directly into the internship Fit Score, so matches become resume-aware
  _Skills are read from the explicit skills list **and** from experience bullets — a bullet saying "rebuilt the ingest path in Go" is evidence, not just a claim. Derived on read, so improving the extractor improves everyone's scores without a re-upload._
- [x] Add a keyword-gap view per internship listing — what is missing vs. that posting
  _Shown on every row. Rendered even with no resume, worded as "this role names…" rather than "not on your resume" — there is nothing to be missing from yet._
- [x] Add resume export (PDF/DOCX download) — _PDF only; DOCX deliberately not done, see below_
  _`ResumeSheet` renders the parsed structure as a document and prints via the same stylesheet approach as the cover letter — no DOM-to-image dependency, no new package. It renders from the editor's live state, so what prints matches what is on screen including unsaved edits; a student can never send a stale copy of the thing they were just looking at. **Single column, real text, ordinary headings — that is the advice, not a style preference.** The critique engine's one honest ATS claim is that *we* are a machine that read this document, and its corollary is that layout is what destroys machine readability; a renderer that reintroduced text boxes or columns would fail the advice we give. Sections with no content are omitted rather than printed as an empty heading, same rule as an honest null everywhere else. **DOCX is not built and needs a decision:** it requires a document-generation dependency, which is the same tradeoff already refused for import ("a zip of XML we cannot read without another dependency") and for PDF export. Print-to-PDF produces real selectable text, so the ATS case is covered; DOCX is worth adding only if students actually ask for it. **A bug this surfaced:** `Nav` and `Footer` live in the root layout and no page could mark them `print-hide`, so every cover letter PDF exported since that feature shipped carried the site navigation across the top and the footer underneath. Fixed in the print stylesheet by element (`body > header`, `body > footer`, `canvas`) and verified under emulated print media._
- [x] Add an overall resume competitiveness summary, not just a per-listing score
  _On `/profile`: roles sharing a skill, roles matched, and the top gaps ranked by how many more roles each would put in reach._

  _`src/lib/resume/critique.ts`. Deterministic, no model call: the parse already produced the structure, so a second LLM pass would only add cost, latency and the chance of inventing a problem that is not there. Every check reports what it counted._
- [x] Surface the critique as specific, actionable fixes per section, not just one score
  _Each finding names the section, quotes the offending bullet where there is one, and says what to change. Findings are ordered by how much the fix moves the score._

  _**Blocked on you.** The line says "the logic you provide" and that logic has not landed yet — paste it in and this becomes buildable. Without it there is nothing here that the critique engine does not already do._
- [ ] Add Smart Resume PDF export
  _Follows the converter. Note this and "resume export" above are the same renderer, built once._
- [x] Build a cover letter editor tied to a specific listing, pulling in the org/role name and key requirements automatically
  _`/listing/[id]` with the full posting facts, the "why this score" breakdown, and the coverage gap line; the editor renders the same grounded context the Fit Score uses._
- [x] Generate a first-draft cover letter grounded in the actual resume-to-listing match data, not generic filler
  _`src/lib/cover-letter/` — context builder (candidate facts, posting facts, ranked evidence, honest gaps) plus the generator. ## Phase 04 — Professional Profiles
*Standing assets outside the platform, built to the same no-slop standard as everything else here.*

- [x] Build a GitHub profile builder for tech-track students — profile README generator and pinned-repo recommendations
  _Both on `/github`, under the audit. `recommendPins` ranks the same repos the audit's showcase dimension grades — so a repo can never be recommended for a slot and simultaneously graded invisible — and prints what each still needs first ("fix first: no README, no topics"). Six, because that is how many slots GitHub gives you._

  _**The README generator is deterministic, and that is a decision rather than a shortcut.** Three reasons, first one decisive: every fact in a README must be *exact*. Repo names, URLs and languages are copied verbatim out of the API response; a model retyping `arc-explore` as `arc_explore` produces a dead link on the first page a recruiter opens and nothing about the output looks wrong enough to catch. A cover letter is prose and needs a writer — a README is a scaffold around facts we already hold. Second, `/github` has no login, and a generate button wired to a model on an unauthenticated form is a bill any visitor can run up. Third, the reason `critique.ts` already gives: a model pass adds cost, latency and the chance of inventing something. Anything we do not hold becomes the same literal `[YOUR SPECIFIC DETAIL: …]` marker the cover letter uses — 7 of them on a bare account — listed back before the copy button, because a student who copies first and reads second publishes brackets. **It deliberately emits no badges, streak counters or trophy widgets:** the audit measures README length after stripping images, so generating the thing we penalise one panel away would be incoherent. Slots are scanned with a stricter pattern than the cover letter's, which matches any bracketed capitalised phrase and would therefore report every markdown link label as a missing fact. Signed in it fills school, major, graduation year and skills from the profile and resume; signed out it is thinner, not broken._
- [x] Add a GitHub profile audit (README quality, repo descriptions, contribution consistency) grounded in what recruiters actually check, not contribution-graph gaming
  _`/github?u=<username>`, open signed-out because everything it reads is already public. Five dimensions: profile README (25), sidebar identity (10), repo descriptions (20), project depth (25), recent activity (20). Deterministic, no model call, same contract as the resume critique — every finding is something we counted off a live API response, and a check we could not run is dropped rather than scored as a miss._

  _**What it refuses to grade, and why that is the point.** The contribution graph and pinned items are GraphQL-only, and GitHub's GraphQL API requires auth for every request — so an unauthenticated audit that graded either would be inventing the number. Pins are therefore *recommended*, never scored; activity is measured from real `pushed_at` timestamps plus the public events feed. That feed is capped at 300 events, so the audit states **the window it could actually observe** rather than claiming 90 days: "active on 1 distinct day in the 1-day window your public event feed covers" is honest where "1 of 90 days" would be fabricated. Not grading the graph is also the advice — it is the one GitHub metric recruiters have learned to discount, and scoring it would be telling students to farm green squares._

  _**The rate limit is the design constraint.** Unauthenticated GitHub is 60 requests/hour **per IP**, and on a hosted deployment that IP is shared by every visitor; one audit costs up to 9 requests, so a naive build gives the whole site ~6 audits an hour. Three responses: `GITHUB_TOKEN` is read if present and raises it to 5,000/hr (no scopes needed — everything read is public; CLAUDE.md says auth is not *required*, which is permission rather than prohibition), responses are cached 15 min via Next's fetch cache, and the optional per-repo README checks **watch the remaining budget as it falls and stop at a reserve of 8** so an audit is never the request that leaves the next visitor with nothing. When they stop early the dimension says so instead of reporting missing READMEs we never looked for. Username parsing is local, so a typo costs zero requests — and it accepts a pasted profile URL, which is what students actually have._

  _**Verified against a real account** (`bennettc1213`, 48/100): correctly found no profile README (404 on the `user/user` repo), `arc-explore` with no description, `rue-noir-coffee` with no README, an empty website field GitHub returns as `""` not null, and all three repos created the same day. It also detects GitHub's unedited starter template ("🔭 I'm currently working on ...") as a **high** severity finding — worse than an empty profile, because it shows the page was started and abandoned — and measures README length after stripping badges and HTML comments, so a wall of shields.io badges measures as the ~0 characters of prose a reader actually gets._
- [x] Build a LinkedIn profile builder for general applicants — headline, About section, and experience bullet templates grounded in real optimization patterns
  _`/linkedin`. Deterministic, and here the reason is sharper than it was for the README: **the checker one panel below penalises clichés** — "passionate", "eager to learn", "team player" — and those are the statistical centre of every LinkedIn About ever written, so a model would emit them sometimes and we would be generating text our own checker fails. A test asserts the generated draft scores ≥80 on both headline and About when run back through `checkLinkedIn`, which is a promise that could not be made about model prose. Headline order is deliberate: subject and school (verifiable at a glance), stack (what recruiter search matches), target last (the part students omit, and the part that gets them filtered *into* a list) — trimmed to LinkedIn's real 220-char cap, since generating something the platform truncates is the same failure the checker flags. The About puts concrete facts in the first paragraph because LinkedIn hides everything past ~3 lines behind "see more". **Bullet templates are grounded rather than generic:** it restates the student's *own* weak resume lines against the pattern — telling someone to "use strong verbs" teaches nothing, the same advice printed above their own sentence is an edit they can make in a minute — and never fills in the number, only marks where one belongs._
- [x] Add a LinkedIn profile checker — scores profile text the student pastes in against headline strength, skills alignment, and recommendations; never fetches a live profile
  _Five dimensions: headline (30), About (25), experience bullets (20), skills (15), recommendations (10). Unstated is dropped, never scored as a miss — "no recommendations stated" is not zero recommendations._

  _**It runs entirely in the browser, which is stronger than the rule requires.** The scoring functions are pure — no db, no network, no import that reaches either — so the pasted profile text is never sent to us at all. There is no endpoint that takes it. For a tool whose whole premise is "we do not fetch your LinkedIn", quietly uploading the same text to score it would be that rule wearing a different hat. **Skills alignment is against our own corpus** and still preserves that: the demand side (top skills across open internships — PowerPoint 262 roles, C++ 176, SQL 127…) is computed server-side and passed *down*, so the comparison happens client-side with nothing going up. Canonicalised on both sides so "js" matches "JavaScript" — reporting a gap they do not have would make every other number suspect._

  _**The highest-value single check is the default headline.** LinkedIn auto-generates "Student at [University]" and most students never change it, while it is the highest-weighted text field for LinkedIn's own search — so it gets its own high-confidence detection rather than being folded into a generic "too short". Experience bullets are graded by importing `isQuantified` and `weakOpener` from `resume/critique.ts` — the same functions, not a second copy: a student told on one page that "Responsible for" is weak and on another that it is fine has been given two products' worth of advice by one product. **The honest limit is printed on the page:** the GitHub audit can say "we fetched your profile and there is no README"; this can only say "the text you gave us has no numbers in it"._
- [x] Add an Open to Work reminder as static advice, not a check — the setting is not visible to a logged-out request
  _On `/linkedin`, and it says why it is advice: the only way to observe the setting is to log in as the student, which is the thing we just promised never to do, so a "check" would be a number we invented. Four items — recruiters-only vs public banner, titles you actually want rather than every title you would accept, an unset start date reading as "now" and quietly excluding you from summer lists built months ahead, and re-checking it after graduation._
- [x] Add a short routing prompt — tech-track students toward GitHub, everyone toward LinkedIn, without forcing both
  _`routePresence` on `/profile`. **"Without forcing both" is the whole design:** one primary call to action and the second offered in a sentence, because two equal buttons is not routing — it hands the decision back to someone who came here to be told. It reads stated interests **and** resume skills, because they disagree in a way that matters: a business major with Python, SQL and React is on the tech track whatever they ticked, and the prompt says the recommendation came from the resume rather than the checkboxes. Either signal suffices, neither is required. **It cuts the other way too** — `product` and `business` are excluded from the tech set, and a student with no technical signal is told plainly that we are *not* going to suggest a GitHub profile, because a design portfolio and a case record are what those tracks are judged on and a GitHub page would be a weekend spent on something their field does not read. Technical evidence means canonical skills outside the `business` group, so Excel and PowerPoint do not qualify; the threshold is 3._
- [x] Let students link a finished GitHub/LinkedIn profile back into their platform profile so it feeds the overall competitiveness summary
  _Migration `0009` adds `github_username` and `linkedin_url`, applied and verified against the live database. **Not folded into `portfolio_url`**, which the roadmap suggested: the resume parser deliberately picks a *non-platform* link for that field, so a GitHub URL would never land there and writing one over it would destroy the personal site. GitHub is stored as **the handle, not the URL** — it is what every API path needs, and storing a URL would mean re-parsing it on every audit — with a pasted profile URL accepted and reduced, since that is what students actually have. `/github` falls back to the saved handle so a signed-in student lands on their own audit rather than an empty form, while an explicit `?u=` still wins so the page stays usable for looking someone else up. The LinkedIn URL is validated as a linkedin.com address and **never fetched by anything** — it is stored so a student can get back to it._

## Phase 05 — Trust, Applications & Tracking
*The failure mode of every aggregator is dead links and stale data. Do not repeat it.*

- [x] Build an application tracker (Saved, Applying, Submitted, Decision)
  _Eight states. `applied_at` is stamped once and never moved; response rate is withheld below ten submissions rather than shown as a flattering fraction._
- [x] Build a one-click application packet _(added; not in the original plan)_
  _`/listing/[id]/apply` — every field an application asks for, assembled from the profile and the parsed resume, with a copy button and **the source printed under each value**. A student pasting a GPA an employer will verify should be able to see whether it came from something they typed or something a parser read off a PDF. Where the two sources disagree the conflict is shown rather than silently resolved (the profile wins as the more recent direct statement, but "your profile says 3.5, your resume says 3.7" appears in accent) — one of them is otherwise about to go onto an application wrong. Email prefers the magic-link account address, which is proven to work, over a resume's printed one._

  _**Attestations are separated and never pre-filled with a guess.** Work authorization, sponsorship, and the race/gender/veteran/disability questions are legal declarations, not form-filling convenience: a wrong answer is a false statement on a form the student signs. Where we hold the answer because they typed it, it is shown back as theirs to confirm; where we do not, we say so. We never store or infer demographics._
- [ ] Auto-submit applications on the student's behalf — **decided against, 2026-08-14**
  _Asked for explicitly ("apply by the click of a button using AI"), and the autofill half was built instead (line above). Submitting is refused on this project's own established rules, not squeamishness. (1) Applications ask what no stored profile answers — "why this team", "describe a time you…" — so a model answering them asserts things about a student that nothing in their resume supports; that is precisely what `cover-letter/generate.ts` uses honest slots to prevent, and it is far worse here because the output reaches an employer under the student's name without them reading it. (2) CLAUDE.md already draws this line for LinkedIn ("never a live fetch, in any form") because LinkedIn sued Proxycurl out of existence; automated submission into Greenhouse/Lever/Workday is the same category of risk, and the precedent here is to read the actual ToS **before** writing code — that is how scholarships.com, niche.com and bold.org were ruled out. (3) Many scholarships require original student work and carry an originality attestation, which makes auto-generated submissions closer to academic fraud than to convenience. Competitors do ship this; employers now filter for it, which is also why mass-apply response rates are poor. The route back in, if wanted, is a browser extension that autofills the real form **in the student's own browser** with them reviewing and clicking submit — same time saved, human still on the attestations._
- [x] Add deadline reminders (email, X days before)

- [x] Build an admin dashboard to review and curate listings before they go live
  _`/admin`. **Deliberately not a review gate, which is what this line asked for.** Every internship here is polled from an employer's own board and claims "confirmed live 5h ago" on that basis — a row waiting in a queue cannot make that claim and would be stale by the time anyone reached it; and a gate applied to the 3,765 rows already in the table empties the feed to zero until one person reviews them all, which would never happen. So the default stays visible and this is **triage**: reports students filed, plus the much smaller set a machine already flagged (`url_dead_strikes >= 2` or `amount_needs_review`, minus anything already reviewed). That is a queue one operator can keep at zero, which is the only kind worth building. It found 3 genuine `amount_needs_review` scholarships on first render._

  _**Access fails closed.** `ADMIN_EMAILS` unset, empty or unparseable means **nobody** is an admin — the tempting "no admins configured so let anyone in during development" shortcut is how an admin panel ships open, and this one can take listings down and read every report. An env var rather than a `profiles.is_admin` column, because a column is a row someone could come to write. It answers **404 rather than redirecting to sign-in**: a login prompt confirms the route exists and is worth attacking. Verified both ways — 404 with nothing configured, and still 404 with an admin configured but no session. Every Server Action re-checks `requireAdmin()` itself, since an action is a public POST endpoint and checking in the page that renders the button is not a check._

- [x] Set up automated dead-link checking on a schedule, flag anything that 404s
  _`npm run check:links`, plus a `check-links` workflow twice daily at a 300-row budget — roughly six days to cover the open corpus, which is the right cadence for a signal whose whole point is patience. The ATS poll was already stronger for internships (`closed_at` within ~20 min of disappearing from the employer's own feed); this covers what that cannot — the ~1,880 scholarship rows we do not poll, and an ATS row whose apply link rots while the feed still lists it._

  _**It flags and never closes, and the first real run proved why.** `closed_at` means the employer stopped listing the job. A 404 is one HTTP response, and responses lie in every direction: 403 is usually a WAF, 429 is a fact about us, 5xx is a bad minute, and a 200 can be a redirect to a generic careers page — the most common way a posting actually dies, and one we cannot see. So **only 404/410 count, and only twice in a row** (the same two-observation rule the UNL crawl still needs). Of the first 400 real URLs checked, 398 answered 200 and **2 answered 403 — both scholarships.com, which is WAF-blocked and perfectly alive.** Had 403 been treated as evidence, that one pass would have flagged all 1,559 scholarships.com rows dead. An inconclusive answer deliberately neither adds a strike nor resets the count, so a flaky host can still accumulate evidence across a 404 → 403 → 404 sequence without the middle answer erasing it. `url_dead_since` is stamped once on the strike that crosses the threshold and never moved, the same rule `applied_at` follows._

  _Politeness is structural rather than a sleep: `interleaveByHost` round-robins the batch so one Greenhouse host is never hit twice in a row, on top of a per-host 1s floor and six workers. 400 rows took 64s. The UI **warns without hiding** — a "link may be dead" marker on the row and a panel on the listing — because we would rather show a labelled uncertain link than bury a live opportunity on two HTTP responses._
- [x] Add a "report this listing" button for students
  _Six reasons, each with a hint, because "wrong details" tells a reviewer nothing without knowing *which* detail and asking at report time beats chasing it later. Ordered by how badly a human is needed rather than by frequency: the first two (dead link, already closed) are things our own checks catch eventually, and the rest are invisible to every automated signal we have. **`asks_for_payment` is the scholarship scam signature** — a legitimate award never charges an application fee — and with `not_real` it sorts to the top of the queue regardless of age, since it is the one report that can cost a student money today. Everything else is oldest-first so nothing rots at the bottom._

  _Collapsed by default and deliberately quiet: a prominent report button on every row invites idle clicking, and a queue full of noise is a queue nobody works. **Sign-in required**, which is a real trade — it loses the passing visitor who clicks a dead link, but that case is now exactly what the link checker catches on its own twice a day, and what only a person can tell us comes from someone engaged enough to have an account. RLS hand-appended to migration `0011`: `authenticated` gets SELECT on **its own rows only**, and INSERT is deliberately not granted, since writes go through a Server Action that has already validated the reason and the posting id. - [x] Write and publish a clear "we do not sell your data" privacy policy
  _`/privacy`, linked from the footer on every page. **Written from `db/schema.ts` and the code that writes to it, not from a template** — the interesting facts about this product are exactly the ones boilerplate smooths over. It names all four services that receive anything (Anthropic gets resume content, and that is stated as the most sensitive thing that leaves; Resend gets an address and a listing title; Supabase; GitHub necessarily learns which username was audited), enumerates what is stored field by field with the feature that reads each one, and lists what we will never do — fetch LinkedIn, store or infer demographics, submit an application, take payment details, sell anything. ## Phase 06 — Engagement & Differentiation
*What makes someone come back a second time.*

- [x] Build an essay/SOP reviewer tool (structured feedback: clarity, relevance, specificity)
  _`/essay`. Four dimensions: answers the prompt (30), specific rather than general (25), clarity (25), structure and length (20). Deterministic, and **it runs entirely in the browser** — pure functions, no endpoint that takes an essay. That is a stronger claim here than it was for the LinkedIn checker: a scholarship essay is frequently about the hardest thing that has happened to someone, and sending it to an API to be graded is something we would have to justify. We cannot justify it for feedback we can produce without it._

  _**Prompt coverage is the differentiated check and the reason the tool exists.** The most common way a scholarship essay fails is not being bad — it is being a *good essay about something else*, usually written for a different application and lightly edited. That is invisible on a reread of your own work and obvious to a reader. So the student pastes the prompt, and we report which of its terms appear nowhere in the essay. Tested against a deliberately weak draft: an essay about "helping others" submitted to a leadership prompt correctly flagged **leadership** and **organization** as missing. **The honest limit is printed beside it** — we match words, not meaning, so an essay covering leadership entirely through the word "captain" reads as a miss; a gap is reported as worth two seconds of checking, never as proof._

  _Two bugs my own tests caught. `stem` did not collapse "challenge" and "challenges", so a prompt asking about challenges would have reported a gap against an essay using the singular — reporting a gap a student does not have is the one error this check cannot make, since it sends them rewriting a section that was already fine. And `isConcrete` counted only digits, so "I rewrote the form four times" read as abstract; students write "three years" far more often than "3 years". **Prompt scaffolding is excluded from coverage** (`describe`, `explain`, `a time`, `for example`): an essay never needs to contain the word "describe", and one obviously silly finding at the top of the list costs trust in all the others._

  _**What it refuses to score is stated on the page:** whether the essay is moving, whether the story is the right one, whether the ending lands. Those decide a scholarship essay and none of them is countable. A clean sheet means the mechanical problems are gone, not that the essay is finished — the page says to give it to one person who knows you and one who does not, and positions itself as the pass before that._

- [x] Add saved searches with new-match alerts
  _Saved from the feed itself, where the filters already are — a saved search is "keep what I just did", and sending someone elsewhere to do it loses the thing they were keeping. No new nav item either, which the nav could not have taken. `npm run alerts`, plus a daily `search-alerts` workflow._

  _**"New" means new to us, and the email says so.** The watermark is `first_seen_at > last_notified_at` — not an employer's stated posting date, which is usually absent and, when present, is a claim rather than an observation. `last_notified_at` defaults to **now** on creation, so a brand-new saved search never fires an alert containing the entire corpus that already matched it, and it moves **only after a send succeeds**: moving it first would let a failed send silently swallow the postings it was going to report._

  _**The `filters` column is jsonb fed from a URL, so it is validated on the way in *and* on the way out.** Only known keys survive `filtersFromParams`; an unrecognised `kind` or `category` falls back to null rather than throwing, so a bad param gives a slightly broader search instead of an error page; and `coerceFilters` is total, so a row written by an older shape degrades field by field — the same `.catch` discipline `resumes.parsed` follows. `limit`, `includeClosed` and `hideBlocked` are deliberately unsaveable: the first two are view preferences, and an alert about *closed* postings is not a thing anyone wants. Saving an empty search is refused, because that is a firehose rather than a search._

  _**Unsubscribing is narrow on purpose.** `?search=<id>` stops that one alert, `?searches=all` stops every alert, and a bare token still stops deadline reminders — three separate things, because clicking "stop alerts for this search" and having it silence everything is the over-reach that makes people unsubscribe from all of it next time. `List-Unsubscribe` points at the per-search link so one-click does what the reader expects. Verified against the live database including the failure paths: a malformed token, a bad search id, and a **valid-format but wrong** token all return false rather than 500ing or silencing someone else's alerts._

  _Dry-run by default like the reminders, and for the same reason — a mistake here lands in someone else's inbox. Exercised end to end against real data: one seeded search, watermark backdated three days, correctly found 25 new matches through the real `auth.users` confirmed-email join._

- [x] Add a side-by-side comparison view for two or three opportunities

  _**A stated value against an unstated one is a difference, not a tie** — that rule shapes the whole page. "One of these publishes a deadline and the other does not" is the difference between an application you can plan and one that could close tomorrow, and rendering both as a quiet dash would hide exactly the thing worth seeing. Unknown is still never scored as a miss; it is just not pretended to be agreement. - [x] Add a weekly email digest of new matches
  _`npm run digest`, plus a weekly `digest` workflow on Sunday mornings. **The cadence lives in `digestDue`, not in the cron** — a fired schedule and a due profile are different facts, so a missed run, a retry or a manual dispatch cannot mail anyone twice in a week._

  _**The thing that stops this being a saved search on a slower clock is that it defers to them.** A saved-search alert answers "what you asked us to watch has news" and requires someone to have already decided what they want; the digest answers "here is what turned up for you" and requires only a profile — which today is everyone, since nobody has saved a search. So anything a student's own notifying searches already cover is subtracted **before** ranking, and the email says how many were withheld and why, rather than leaving an unexplained short list. The covered set is built by running their searches through the same `getFeed` the alert job uses rather than restating the filters — one taxonomy, one judgement. **The window is `max(search.createdAt, lastDigestAt)`:** a search alerts on everything first seen since it was created (its watermark starts at creation and only advances on a successful send), and clamping to the digest window keeps the query bounded. Verified both directions against live data — a search created *just now* correctly covers nothing, and one created ten days ago covered all 288 scholarships in the pool, leaving a digest of six internships._

  _**A live dry run rewrote the selection rule.** The first one produced six scholarships and zero internships for a profile stating software, data/ai, product and business — and the cause is structural, not luck. A scholarship is scored on three dimensions and can be known on all three, reaching a confident 100; an internship is scored on five, with `term` and `skills` routinely unstated, so a strong one sits at 3-of-5 and `rankingScore` correctly shrinks it toward the prior. Best scholarship that run: **100.0. Best internship: 82.0** — every internship loses every time. `rankingScore` does its job; what it cannot do is make "known on 3 of 3" and "known on 3 of 5" mean the same thing. So the digest holds `MIN_PER_KIND` slots back rather than pretending the cross-kind ordering is good enough to award all six. The feed does not need this because it shows everything; a six-line email is where the asymmetry becomes "we never mentioned a single internship". The underlying asymmetry is recorded in FIXES._

  _Quality floor at 50 — `score/fit.ts`'s neutral prior — applied to the confidence-weighted rank, not the displayed score: the digest may only recommend something that looks **better than knowing nothing**. Blocked rows are dropped outright, which is where this parts from the feed (there they stay visible because a stated requirement is real information); six lines is no place for one the student cannot apply to. **A quiet week sends nothing** — an empty profile, no new rows, or nothing above the floor each end in silence, and the job prints which, because a run reporting "sent 0" and nothing else cannot be told from a broken one. Every score in the email carries its `on N of M` marker, per `FitResult`'s own rule: a recommendation is the last place that may quietly go missing._

  _**Two things this shipped alongside, both found by running it.** The unsubscribe page had been promising "you can turn reminders back on from your profile" since reminders shipped, and there was no such control anywhere — `/profile` now has an email panel, and it is a **separate action from the profile form on purpose**: every unsubscribe link writes those columns without a session, so folding them into the profile form would let a stale tab silently resubscribe someone who had opted out. And the covered-set lookup was silently truncating at `getFeed`'s default 500, which showed up as 237 of 288 scholarships covered — truncation there does not fail loudly, it just lets a duplicate into an inbox._
